The research idea

Persistent structure as confined wave dynamics.

The homepage follows the proposed dependency flow: a chaotic broadband field organizes its first ZW1 fold around the invariant zero-wave reference, Lyapunov basins draw down the field state, finite-wavenumber selection filters the spectrum, a toroidal Phase–Flux grid routes energy and phase subject to causal-flow constraints, eigenmode attractors emerge, and resonant geometry closes into curvature locking.

01 / 08 · Zero-wave turbulence → ZW1 fold

Illustrative dependency map in the WCT visual language. “Sinkhole” is used here as a visual metaphor for mode-attractor basins; individual stages retain their separate proof, numerical, or proposal status.

  1. 01

    Zero-wave turbulence → ZW1 fold

    Chaotic broadband wave turbulence folds toward a first coherent ZW1 mode in the three spatial directions. The exactly invariant zero-wave state remains the mathematical reference, distinct from the fluctuating field drawn here; the Sobolev n ≤ 3 condition belongs to the boundedness argument in step 03.

  2. 02

    Lyapunov basin flow

    Field states are shown moving through a basin of lower 𝓔_WCT[ψ], not merely down a one-dimensional curve. Under the stated gradient-flow assumptions, trajectories descend toward lower-energy attractor structure.

  3. 03

    Sobolev three-dimensional bound

    1–3D: the H² embedding controls peaks. 4D: a critical multiscale sequence develops a growing peak despite a bounded H² norm. 5–6D: Gaussian concentration grows supercritical. The n-cube projection shows dimensional geometry, not physical confinement failure.

    Dimensional-bound derivation ↗ · Related eigenmode study ↗

  4. 04

    Finite-k shell selection

    Watch broadband Fourier modes outside the preferred nonzero wavenumber fade under a quartic spectral filter. The radial-power curve sharpens around k★; this is an illustrative filter response, not a WCT PDE integration.

  5. 05

    Phase–Flux organization

    Energy density u, flux S, and phase θ are illustrated on a toroidal grid with a central spindle-like flow channel. The full capacity rail represents the null-flow normalization, not a measured flux or a solved PFF state. Phase–Flux paper ↗

  6. 06

    Self-emergent eigenmode sinkholes

    Broadband content is visualized draining into competing mode basins—sinkhole-like attractors—while spectral entropy falls and a smaller set of persistent eigenmode-like structures dominates.

  7. 07

    Resonant confinement

    Counterpropagating waves reflect at idealized boundaries and interfere into a standing mode. Nodes stay fixed as antinodes oscillate; zero average outward flux applies to the ideal cavity shown, not a proven WCT confinement solution.

  8. 08

    Curvature locking

    Phase advances around a curved closed path with integer winding. An illustrated feedback correction drives the seam mismatch toward zero while the tangent and osculating circle track local geometry. The decay is prescribed, not a demonstrated WCT stability law.

About

Engineering practice and independent research.

Richard J. Reyes is a controls engineer and software developer working in industrial refrigeration and process automation. His engineering work includes PLC logic, instrumentation, alarms, sequencing, networked HMIs, commissioning, and control-system troubleshooting.

He holds a B.S. in Computer Science from San José State University. His independent research spans nonlinear wave dynamics, mathematical physics, scientific computing, experimental signal analysis, public-dataset studies, computational architecture, and resonance-based control.
