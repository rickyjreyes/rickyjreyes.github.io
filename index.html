The research idea

Persistent structure as confined wave dynamics.

Wave Confinement Theory develops a dependency path from an invariant zero-wave state through Lyapunov descent, Sobolev regularity, finite-wavenumber selection, the Phase–Flux substrate, self-emergent eigenmodes, resonant confinement, and curvature locking.

01 / 08 · Zero-wave invariant state

Illustrative dependency map. Individual stages have different proof, simulation, or proposal status; the sequence itself is not an empirical result.

  1. 01

    Zero-wave invariant state

    The substrate begins from a distinguished zero-wave state that remains invariant rather than generating structure from nothing.

  2. 02

    Lyapunov descent

    A candidate energy functional supplies a direction of descent under the stated negative-gradient-flow assumptions.

  3. 03

    Sobolev confinement bound

    The regularity route uses \(H^2(\mathbb{R}^n)\hookrightarrow L^\infty\) to restrict the stated bounded-confinement argument to \(n\le 3\).

  4. 04

    Finite-\(k\) selection

    A quartic band-pass selects a preferred nonzero wavenumber \(k_\star\), producing shell-like spectral support rather than unrestricted growth.

  5. 05

    Phase–Flux substrate

    Observable energy density, flux, and phase organize the selected band into a conservation- and causal-flow-aware field description.

  6. 06

    Self-emergent eigenmodes

    Numerical broadband or random fields evolve toward finite-band support, reduced spectral entropy, mode competition, and eigenmode-like organization.

  7. 07

    Resonant confinement

    Boundary and feedback conditions organize persistent modes.

  8. 08

    Curvature locking

    Phase, geometry, and topology stabilize localized structure.

Research status. WCT is an independently developed research program spanning mathematical formulation, numerical simulation, experiment, and open-data analysis. Equation build-up · registered chain

From a closed curve to an inverse-length scale.

Curvature and torsion of a closed curve Γ combine into a spectral rate; its weighted average sets the effective wavenumber; under the stated E5 conditions, that maps dimensionally to a mass scale.

Curvature spectral rate · E1B
\[\sigma(s)=\sqrt{\kappa(s)^2+\tau(s)^2}\]
Effective-wavenumber chain · E5 assumptions
\[k_{\mathrm{wind}}=k_{\sigma}=\langle\sigma\rangle_w\]
Mass–curvature dimensional mapping · E6
\[m=\frac{\hbar}{c}\,k_{\mathrm{eff}}\]
\[\text{under E5:}\quad m=\frac{\hbar}{c}\,\langle\sigma\rangle_w\]

About

Engineering practice and independent research.

Richard J. Reyes is a controls engineer and software developer working in industrial refrigeration and process automation. His engineering work includes PLC logic, instrumentation, alarms, sequencing, networked HMIs, commissioning, and control-system troubleshooting.

He holds a B.S. in Computer Science from San José State University. His independent research spans nonlinear wave dynamics, mathematical physics, scientific computing, experimental signal analysis, public-dataset studies, computational architecture, and resonance-based control.
