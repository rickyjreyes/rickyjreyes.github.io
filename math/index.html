A connected example: fourth-order nonlinear waves

From an energy inequality to stability.

For one two-dimensional cubic–quintic model, four separate theorems establish a connected path. The parameters and function spaces matter: this is not a statement about every nonlinear wave equation.

01Binding→02Existence→03Global evolution→04Stability
01 · Energy bindingmath / StrictBinding.lean

Why the minimizing wave cannot gain by splitting.

Splitting a fixed mass into two positive portions always raises the sum of the minimum energies, within the proved parameter range.

\[I(m)<I(\mu)+I(m-\mu)\]

Why this matters

This strict energy gap is the binding step used when ruling out fragmentation of a minimizing sequence.

Exact assumptions

In the two-dimensional cubic–quintic setting: β, γ > 0; 0 < m ≤ mB; and every 0 < μ < m.

The key step of the proof

Apply the strict gap estimate to both portions of mass. Compare the energies per unit mass, then add them using μ + (m − μ) = m.

Scope: Two-dimensional cubic–quintic model with β, γ > 0 and 0 < m ≤ mB, not an all-mass theorem.

02 · Existencemath / WholeSpaceMinimizers.lean

Does a lowest-energy wave actually exist?

The lowest constrained energy is achieved by at least one actual H² field, not merely approached by a sequence.

\[\exists U:\quad M(U)=m,\quad E(U)=I(m)\]

Why this matters

Attainment establishes a nonempty minimizing set on which the later orbital-stability statement is formulated.

Exact assumptions

Two-dimensional model; β, γ > 0; 0 < m ≤ mB. The result does not select a unique profile.

The key step of the proof

Construct a sequence of fields approaching the infimum. Compactness supplies a limit preserving both the mass and the minimum energy.

Scope: Whole-space minimizer set with compactness modulo translations, not profile uniqueness.

03 · Evolutionmath / CubicQuinticFlow.lean

Can that wave evolve for all time?

Every allowed H² initial state has exactly one solution defined for all positive and negative times.

\[\forall u_0\in H^2(\mathbb R^2),\quad \exists!\,u:\mathbb R\to H^2(\mathbb R^2)\]

Why this matters

Global existence and uniqueness supply the evolution needed by the subsequent stability theorem.

Exact assumptions

The specific two-dimensional fourth-order cubic–quintic evolution with γ > 0 and initial data in H².

The key step of the proof

Solve the global evolution in a transformed Hilbert-space representation, map it back to H², then use uniqueness of the transformed ODE.

Scope: Model-specific flow, not a general fourth-order PDE existence theorem.

04 · Stabilitymath / OrbitalStability.lean

What happens under a small perturbation?

Starting sufficiently close to the set of energy minimizers keeps the solution close to that entire set for all time.

\[\operatorname{dist}_{H^2}(u(0),\mathcal G_m)<\delta\ \Longrightarrow\ \sup_t\operatorname{dist}_{H^2}(u(t),\mathcal G_m)<\varepsilon\]

Why this matters

The theorem turns variational information into a dynamical stability statement, without choosing a unique wave profile.

Exact assumptions

The specified 2D equation, β, γ > 0, 0 < m ≤ mB, with distance measured in H².

The key step of the proof

A variational stability estimate supplies the tolerance δ. Conservation of mass and energy then controls distance from the entire minimizing set for all times.

Scope: Set stability in the sufficient mass range, not uniqueness or stability of every excited state.

05 · Inverse problemsmath / V18Identifiability.lean

How much can measurements fail to identify?

Nearby, genuinely different parameter choices can give the same eleven observations.

\[\dim(\text{local observation fiber})=20\]

Why this matters

The local twenty-dimensional fiber shows structural ambiguity, beyond numerical ill-conditioning.

Exact assumptions

The frozen 31-parameter / 11-observation nonlinear map, with α ≥ 0.

The key step of the proof

The frozen Jacobian has an eleven-dimensional image and twenty-dimensional kernel. The local level-set theorem turns that kernel into a nonlinear family of indistinguishable parameters.

Scope: Local to the frozen model, not a global identification classification.

06 · Geometric obstructionmath / ManifoldScalarMetric.lean

Can one complex scalar define a four-dimensional metric?

A complex scalar's real-gradient Gram tensor has rank at most two on a four-dimensional tangent space.

\[\operatorname{rank}(g_\psi)\leq2,\qquad\dim\ker(g_\psi)\geq2\]

Why this matters

This particular construction cannot define a nondegenerate Lorentzian metric, without ruling out other metric constructions.

Exact assumptions

A C¹ complex scalar on the specified four-dimensional smooth manifold.

The key step of the proof

The theorem combines Gram symmetry and nonnegativity with a rank-at-most-two bound. In dimension four that leaves a nontrivial kernel, so this tensor is degenerate.

Scope: Applies to this specific tensor construction, not all scalar-derived effective metrics.
